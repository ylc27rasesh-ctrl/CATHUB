Module 00 · Orientation

Work, time,
and the rate
that connects them.

A teacher's overview. Time and work is one of the most patterned chapters in CAT — a small set of techniques solves almost every question, but the verbal disguises are endless. Master the LCM trick and the work-as-units reframe, and you handle the entire topic.

Time and work is structurally identical to Volume VI's distance-speed-time triangle. Work = Rate × Time is the same algebraic identity as Distance = Speed × Time. The only difference is what's moving: instead of an object covering distance, a worker (or pipe) completes a job. Once you see this isomorphism, the entire chapter inherits the techniques you already drilled in Volume VI.

What makes time-and-work harder than it should be is the verbal density. "Anil can paint a house in 60 days while Bimal can paint it in 84 days. Anil starts painting and after 10 days, Bimal and Charu join him…" — every CAT problem reads like a soap opera. The discipline this codex teaches: convert the prose into a units-of-work table before doing anything else. Then arithmetic.

0.1How to use this codex in class

The nine modules are sequenced for a 6–8 hour block. Module 1 introduces the work-rate-time triangle. Module 2 covers the LCM trick — the single most important shortcut. Module 3 handles combined work and wage-sharing. Module 4 covers phased problems (where workers join or leave mid-task). Module 5 is pipes and cisterns, which is just time-and-work with one worker negative.

Teaching philosophy. Every time-and-work problem becomes trivial once you assume the total work equals the LCM of the individual times. Then everyone's daily output becomes an integer. Then the problem becomes arithmetic. Drill this transformation until automatic.

0.2What the CAT actually asks

  • Two workers together. A in x days, B in y days; together in how many?
  • Three workers, mid-task transitions. One starts, two join after some days, all finish. CAT 2021 Anil-Bimal-Charu archetype.
  • Wage-sharing in proportion to work. Total wages distributed in proportion to units of work done.
  • Backward calculation. Combined time given; find one worker's individual time.
  • Efficiency variations. "B is twice as efficient as A" — direct ratio of work-rates.
  • Pipe systems with inlets and outlets. Some pipes fill (positive rate), some drain (negative rate).
  • Pipes with time-delayed opening. Pipe A opens at 2pm; B at 3pm; tank fills at 10pm — CAT 2024 pattern.

Classroom diagnostic in two minutes. Ask: "A can do a job in 20 days, B in 30 days. How long do they take together?" The fluent student sets total work = LCM(20, 30) = 60 units. A does 3 units/day, B does 2 units/day, together 5 units/day. Time = 60/5 = 12 days. Twenty seconds. The struggling student adds 1/20 + 1/30 longhand. Use the diagnostic to identify how much of Module 2 needs to be drilled.

Module 01 · The Frame

Work, time,
and rate.

The same triangle as Volume VI, with different labels. Work = Rate × Time. Three quantities, one identity, every CAT problem in this chapter.

1.1The core identity

The Work Triangle Work = Rate × Time
Equivalently: Rate = Work / Time and Time = Work / Rate

Rate is work-per-unit-time. If A finishes a job in n days, A's rate is 1/n of the job per day.

1.2The "fraction of work per day" framing

Standard textbook approach: if A does the job in n days, A's rate is 1/n per day. If B does it in m days, B's rate is 1/m. Together: rate = 1/n + 1/m. Combined time: 1 / (1/n + 1/m) = nm/(n+m).

Two-Worker Combined Formula A finishes in n days, B in m days.
Together they finish in nm/(n + m) days

Worked: A in 20 days, B in 30 days. Together = 20 × 30/(20 + 30) = 600/50 = 12 days.

1.3Why this gets ugly fast

The fraction approach works for two workers. For three or more, the algebra becomes a mess of LCMs. There's a much cleaner technique, which we'll cover in Module 2.

1.4The two variation patterns

For fixed work

Rate and time are inversely proportional. Double the rate, halve the time. Same as fixed-distance speed-time inverse from Vol. VI.

For fixed time

Rate and work are directly proportional. Double the rate, double the work done in the fixed period.

1.5Worked walkthrough

Foundation example — two workers togetherEasy

A can complete a task in 12 days, and B can complete it in 18 days. How long will they take together?

FORMULAnm/(n+m) = 12 × 18/(12+18) = 216/30 = 7.2 days.
Answer7.2 days
· In-class drill ·

Mental math, ten seconds each:

A: 6 days, B: 12 days, together? · A: 10 days, B: 15 days, together? · A: 8 days, B: 24 days, together?

Answers: 4 days · 6 days · 6 days.

Module 02 · The Master Technique

The LCM trick.

The single most powerful technique in this entire chapter. Assume total work equals the LCM of individual completion times, and every worker's daily output becomes a clean integer. The fractions vanish.

2.1The trick — explained

Standard approach: A's rate = 1/12, B's rate = 1/18. Combined rate = 1/12 + 1/18 = 3/36 + 2/36 = 5/36. Total time = 36/5 = 7.2 days. Three fractions, two LCMs, mental friction.

The LCM trick: assume the total work equals LCM(12, 18) = 36 units. Then A does 36/12 = 3 units/day. B does 36/18 = 2 units/day. Combined: 5 units/day. Total time = 36/5 = 7.2 days. Same answer, no fractions until the very last step.

The LCM Trick Set total work W = LCM of individual completion times.
Each worker's daily output = W / their individual time.
Combined rate = sum of individual daily outputs.
Combined time = W / (combined rate).

2.2Why this scales

For two workers, the fraction approach is fine. For three or more, the LCM trick is dramatically cleaner. Three workers in 12, 18, 24 days: LCM = 72. Daily outputs: 6, 4, 3. Total daily output: 13. Combined time: 72/13. Try doing this with fractions and you'll add 1/12 + 1/18 + 1/24 = 6/72 + 4/72 + 3/72 = 13/72 — same answer, but the LCM approach is faster and more reliable.

2.3Worked applications

Three workers togetherMedium

A, B and C can complete a piece of work in 15, 20, and 30 days respectively. How long do they take together?

LCMW = LCM(15, 20, 30) = 60 units.
A's rate = 4 units/day. B's rate = 3. C's rate = 2. Combined = 9.
Time = 60/9 = 20/3 ≈ 6.67 days.
Answer20/3 ≈ 6.67 days

2.4Backward problems

Find one individual time given combinedMedium

A and B together complete a task in 10 days. A alone takes 15 days. How long does B take alone?

METHODTake total work = LCM(10, 15) = 30 units. Combined rate = 30/10 = 3 units/day. A's rate = 30/15 = 2 units/day. So B's rate = 3 − 2 = 1 unit/day. B alone = 30/1 = 30 days.
Answer30 days

2.5Efficiency ratios

"B is twice as efficient as A" means B's rate is twice A's rate. Equivalently, A takes twice as long as B. If A takes 30 days alone, B takes 15.

"A is 50% more efficient than B" means A's rate = 1.5 × B's rate. So A's time = (2/3) × B's time.

Module 03 · Wage Distribution

Combined work
& wages.

When multiple workers complete a task and are paid a fixed total. Wages should be distributed in proportion to the work each actually did — not their share of total time.

3.1The wage-sharing rule

Wage-Sharing Total wages W split among n workers.
Worker i's wage = W × (units of work i did) / (total units of work)

Equivalently, wage is proportional to work done.

Work done by worker i = (daily rate) × (days worked). For a worker who joins mid-task and leaves before completion, count only the days they worked.

3.2The CAT 2021 Anil-Bimal-Charu problem

CAT 2021 — Anil, Bimal, Charu paint a houseHard

Anil can paint a house in 60 days while Bimal can paint it in 84 days. Anil starts painting and after 10 days, Bimal and Charu join him. Together, they complete the painting in 14 more days. If they are paid a total of ₹21,000 for the job, find the share of Charu, in INR, proportionate to the work done by him.

LCMW = LCM(60, 84) = 420 units.
Anil's rate = 7 units/day. Bimal's rate = 5 units/day.
WORK DONE BY EACHAnil worked 10 + 14 = 24 days → 168 units.
Bimal worked 14 days → 70 units.
Charu worked 14 days but his rate is unknown. Total = 420 units, so Charu = 420 − 168 − 70 = 182 units.
WAGE SHARECharu's share = 21,000 × 182/420 = 21,000 × 13/30 = 9,100.
Answer₹9,100

Teaching note: the LCM trick converts 1/60, 1/84 into clean integers 7, 5. The wage share is then a simple fraction of the total. Without the LCM trick this problem takes twice as long.

3.3The CAT 2021 Anil-Barun-Chandu variant

CAT 2021 — Anil, Barun, Chandu paint togetherHard

Anil can paint a house in 12 days while Barun can paint it in 16 days. Anil, Barun, and Chandu together complete the painting in 6 days and are paid ₹24,000 in total. Find Chandu's share, proportionate to his work.

LCMW = LCM(12, 16) = 48 units. Anil's rate = 4 units/day, Barun's rate = 3 units/day.
WORK DONEAll three worked 6 days. Anil did 24, Barun did 18. Together they did 48 units in 6 days, so combined rate = 8 units/day. Chandu's rate = 8 − 4 − 3 = 1 unit/day. Chandu did 6 units in 6 days.
SHAREChandu = 24,000 × 6/48 = 3,000.
Answer₹3,000

Module 04 · Join & Leave

Workers join
and leave
mid-task.

The most-tested CAT archetype. One worker starts. Others join. Some leave. The total work is completed at some point. The technique is to track work-done as a running sum and equate to total.

4.1The standard pattern

A starts. After x days, B joins. After y more days, the work is done. Compute A's work in x days plus combined work in y days, equate to total.

Phased Work Identity Total work = Σ (rate of active worker(s)) × (days at that rate)

Convert to LCM units, track each phase's contribution, sum to total work.

4.2Worked applications

CAT 2019 — Anil, Sunil, BimalHard

Anil alone can do a job in 20 days while Sunil alone can do it in 40 days. Anil starts the job, and after 3 days, Sunil joins him. Again, after a few more days, Bimal joins them and they together finish the job. If Bimal has done 10% of the job, find the total number of days the job took.

LCMW = LCM(20, 40) = 40 units. Anil = 2 units/day. Sunil = 1 unit/day.
WORK IN PHASESBimal does 10% × 40 = 4 units. Anil's first 3 days alone: 6 units. So Anil + Sunil together do 40 − 6 − 4 = 30 units.
DURATION OF PHASE 2Anil + Sunil rate = 3 units/day. Time = 30/3 = 10 days.
TOTAL TIME3 + 10 = 13 days.
Answer13 days

Teaching note: Bimal does 4 units, which all happens during the same phase as Anil + Sunil together. The "10% by Bimal" is a constraint that pins down when he joined — though the problem only asks total time, not when.

4.3The CAT 2021 Rahul-Gautam problem

CAT 2021 — Rahul and Gautam at 9 AMHard

Rahul started a work at 9 AM and Gautam joined him two hours later. They worked together and completed the work at 5 PM the same day. If both had started at 9 AM and worked together throughout, the work would have been completed 30 minutes earlier. Working alone, how many hours would Rahul have taken to complete the work?

SETUPTotal time from 9 AM to 5 PM = 8 hours. Rahul alone for 2 hours, then both for 6 hours. If both started together, work would take 8 − 0.5 = 7.5 hours.
VARIABLESLet Rahul's rate = r, Gautam's rate = g.
Scenario 1: 2r + 6(r + g) = 1 ⟹ 8r + 6g = 1.
Scenario 2: 7.5(r + g) = 1 ⟹ r + g = 2/15.
SOLVEFrom scenario 2: g = 2/15 − r. Sub into 1: 8r + 6(2/15 − r) = 1 ⟹ 8r + 12/15 − 6r = 1 ⟹ 2r = 1 − 4/5 = 1/5 ⟹ r = 1/10.
Rahul alone takes 1/(1/10) = 10 hours.
Answer10 hours

4.4The "alternate day" pattern

Some CAT problems have workers operating on alternate days, or every third day, etc. Track each pair-of-days (or triple-of-days) as a unit cycle and compute how many cycles plus a partial cycle complete the work.

Module 05 · Tanks & Taps

Pipes and
cisterns.

Time and work, with one worker negative. Inlet pipes fill (positive rate); outlet pipes drain (negative rate). Same LCM technique, same arithmetic, with a sign change. Master this and the chapter is finished.

5.1The framework

An inlet pipe filling a tank in n hours has rate 1/n (positive). An outlet (drain) pipe emptying in m hours has rate 1/m (positive in magnitude, but counted negative in the system). When all pipes operate together, the net rate is the sum of inlet rates minus outlet rates.

Pipes & Cisterns — The Rule Net rate = Σ inlet rates − Σ outlet rates

Time to fill (with net positive rate) = Tank capacity / Net rate.
If net rate ≤ 0, the tank never fills.

5.2The LCM trick reapplied

Same as Module 2: assume tank capacity = LCM of individual pipe times. Each pipe's rate becomes a clean integer. Net rate is integer arithmetic.

Basic pipe combinationEasy

Two pipes A and B fill a cistern in 20 and 30 minutes respectively. A third pipe C empties it in 40 minutes. How long does it take to fill the cistern when all three are opened together?

LCMW = LCM(20, 30, 40) = 120. A = 6, B = 4, C = −3 units/min. Net = 7 units/min.
TIME120/7 ≈ 17.14 minutes.
Answer≈ 120/7 minutes

5.3The CAT 2024 timing-staggered problem

CAT 2024 — pipes A and B with staggered timingElite

Two pipes A and B are attached to an empty water tank. Pipe A fills the tank while pipe B drains it. If pipe A is opened at 2 PM and pipe B is opened at 3 PM, then the tank becomes full at 10 PM. Instead, if pipe A is opened at 2 PM and pipe B is opened at 4 PM, then the tank becomes full at 6 PM. If pipe B is not opened at all, find the time (in minutes) taken to fill the tank.

SETUPLet A's rate = a (units/hr), B's drain rate = b (units/hr). Tank capacity = T.
Scenario 1: A operates 8 h (2 PM – 10 PM), B operates 7 h (3 PM – 10 PM). 8a − 7b = T.
Scenario 2: A operates 4 h (2 PM – 6 PM), B operates 2 h (4 PM – 6 PM). 4a − 2b = T.
SOLVEFrom the two equations: 8a − 7b = 4a − 2b ⟹ 4a = 5b ⟹ b = 4a/5.
Sub into second: 4a − 2(4a/5) = T ⟹ 4a − 8a/5 = T ⟹ 12a/5 = T.
So if A alone, time = T/a = 12/5 hours = 144 minutes.
Answer144 minutes

5.4Pipes with leaks

"A pipe fills a tank in 20 min, but a leak causes it to take 25 min." The leak is functioning as a drain pipe. Compute its effective drain rate from the slowdown, then ask further questions.

Setup: A's rate = 1/20. Net rate (with leak) = 1/25. Leak rate = 1/20 − 1/25 = 5/500 − 4/500 = 1/100. So the leak alone empties the tank in 100 minutes.

5.5System of n pipes — Diophantine

An interesting CAT-mock pattern: "n pipes (some fill, some drain) together fill the tank in 5 hours. How many possible values of n?" Set up the linear equation between fill and drain pipes; require integer solutions. The answer is usually a parametric family.

Module 06 · Applications

Where work
actually lives
in CAT.

Six recurring archetypes. Each disguises the same triangle in different prose. Recognise the archetype, apply the LCM trick, compute.

6.1Theme — Painters and houses

The Anil/Bimal/Barun/Charu/Chandu archetype. CAT loves this framing. Multiple workers, multi-phase, wage distribution. Always solve with the LCM trick.

6.2Theme — Pipes and tanks

Module 5 covered this in full. Inlets vs outlets, timing variations, leaks.

6.3Theme — Workdays and hours-per-day

CAT 2024 — Renu and Seema with hours-per-dayHard

Renu would take 15 days working 4 hours per day to complete a certain task whereas Seema would take 8 days working 5 hours per day to complete the same task. They decide to work together. Seema agrees to work for double the number of hours per day as Renu, while Renu agrees to work for double the number of days as Seema. If Renu works 2 hours per day, find the number of days Seema will work.

SETUPTotal work in hours: Renu's perspective: 15 × 4 = 60 person-hours. Seema's perspective: 8 × 5 = 40 person-hours. So Seema is more efficient per hour.
Renu's rate = 1/60 of the task per hour. Seema's rate = 1/40 per hour.
NEW SCHEDULERenu works 2 hrs/day for d_R days. Seema works 4 hrs/day for d_S days. Constraint: d_R = 2 × d_S.
Total work: (1/60) × 2 × d_R + (1/40) × 4 × d_S = 1.
Substitute d_R = 2 d_S: (2/60)(2 d_S) + (4/40) d_S = 1 ⟹ (4 d_S)/60 + d_S/10 = 1 ⟹ d_S/15 + d_S/10 = 1.
SOLVE(2 d_S + 3 d_S)/30 = 1 ⟹ 5 d_S = 30 ⟹ d_S = 6.
AnswerSeema works 6 days

6.4Theme — Workers with efficiency differences

"A is twice as efficient as B" or "C does the work in half the time of B". Convert to rate ratios and proceed.

6.5Theme — Garrison/ration problems

"There is enough food for n soldiers for d days. After x days, more soldiers arrive…" Mathematically identical to a work problem. Food-consumption-rate × time = total food. When numbers change, recompute.

6.6Theme — Three-pair problems

Three-pair Amar-Akbar-AnthonyHard

Working together Amar and Akbar can complete a project in 1 year, Akbar and Anthony can complete in 16 months, Anthony and Amar can complete in 2 years. The person who is neither the fastest nor the slowest works alone — find the time he will take to complete the project.

SETUPExpress times in months. Amar + Akbar = 12 months ⟹ a + b = 1/12. Akbar + Anthony = 16 months ⟹ b + c = 1/16. Anthony + Amar = 24 months ⟹ c + a = 1/24.
SUMAdd all three: 2(a + b + c) = 1/12 + 1/16 + 1/24 = (4 + 3 + 2)/48 = 9/48 = 3/16.
So a + b + c = 3/32.
INDIVIDUAL RATESa = (3/32) − (1/16) = (3 − 2)/32 = 1/32 ⟹ Amar alone = 32 months.
b = (3/32) − (1/24) = (9 − 4)/96 = 5/96 ⟹ Akbar = 96/5 = 19.2 months.
c = (3/32) − (1/12) = (9 − 8)/96 = 1/96 ⟹ Anthony = 96 months.
MIDDLE WORKERTimes: Amar 32, Akbar 19.2, Anthony 96. Fastest = Akbar (19.2). Slowest = Anthony. Middle = Amar at 32 months.
Answer32 months

Module 07 · Tactics

Twelve tips,
tricks, and traps.

The accumulated wisdom — what to drill, what to whisper, and the predictable errors students make under pressure.

TIP 01 · LCM
Set total work = LCM

Always. Daily outputs become integers, arithmetic vanishes. The single most useful technique in this chapter.

TIP 02 · TABLE
Build a work-units table

For multi-worker problems with phases, draw a table of phases × workers × units done. Visual clarity beats algebra.

TIP 03 · TRIANGLE
Work = Rate × Time

Same identity as distance-speed-time from Vol. VI. Choose which two to use and solve for the third.

TIP 04 · EFFICIENCY
Twice as efficient = half the time

Convert "efficiency ratios" to rate ratios. If A is k× efficient, A's rate is k× B's rate. A's time = (1/k) × B's time.

TIP 05 · WAGES
Wages proportional to work done

Not proportional to days worked. Compute each worker's units, divide by total units, multiply by total wages.

TIP 06 · PIPES SIGN
Outlet pipes are negative

Subtract outlet rates from inlet rates. If net is zero or negative, the tank never fills (or empties forever).

TIP 07 · TWO-WORKER FORMULA
nm/(n+m) for two workers together

Quick mental check for simple problems. Don't apply to 3+ workers.

TIP 08 · PHASED
Track work by phase, sum to 1

For workers who join or leave: total work = Σ (rate × time in each phase).

TIP 09 · HOURS-PER-DAY
Convert to person-hours

When workers operate at different daily durations, multiply rate × hours-per-day to get true daily output.

TIP 10 · LEAK
Leak = drain pipe in disguise

Slowdown × original time gives the leak's drain time alone. Same arithmetic as outlet pipes.

TIP 11 · UNITS
Match units consistently

Days, hours, minutes — pick one and convert everything. Don't mix.

TIP 12 · SANITY
Verify with one phase

After solving, check that total work in each phase sums to the expected total. Catches sign errors and missed contributions.

7.1Five traps

Trap 1 — Days vs hours. "A works 4 hours/day for 15 days; B works 5 hours/day for 8 days." Different hours per day. Convert to total person-hours before computing rates.

Trap 2 — Wages by days, not by work. If A worked 5 days and B worked 10 days but B is twice as efficient, they did equal work. Wages are equal.

Trap 3 — Outlet pipe forgotten. "Three pipes A, B, C..." Always check whether C is fill or drain. If drain, sign is negative.

Trap 4 — Misreading "twice as efficient". Means twice the rate, half the time. Read aloud to confirm direction.

Trap 5 — Phased work without LCM. Trying to track fractions across phases. The fractions don't add cleanly. Use LCM units.

7.2The two-minute checklist

  1. Read twice. Identify all workers and their individual times.
  2. Compute LCM of all individual times. Set total work = LCM units.
  3. Compute each worker's units/day.
  4. For phased problems, track work done per phase.
  5. For wages, divide by total units, multiply by total wages.
  6. For pipes, subtract outlet rates.
  7. Verify: do all phases sum to total work?

A starts a task that he can complete alone in 24 days. After 6 days, B joins. They finish in 8 more days. How long would B alone take?

METHODLCM = 60. Rates 3 + 2 + 1 = 6. Time = 60/6 = 10 min.

Q9 · Garrison foodMedium

A garrison has food for 1000 soldiers for 30 days. After 5 days, 200 more soldiers arrive. How long will the food now last?

METHODInitial food = 30,000 soldier-days. After 5 days, food consumed = 5,000. Remaining = 25,000. Soldiers = 1,200. Days left = 25,000/1,200 ≈ 20.83.
Answer≈ 20.83 days
Q10 · Pipe with leakMedium

A pipe fills a tank in 12 minutes. Due to a leak, it now takes 15 minutes. How long would the leak alone take to empty the full tank?

METHODInlet rate = 1/12. Net rate (with leak) = 1/15. Leak rate = 1/12 − 1/15 = (5−4)/60 = 1/60. So leak alone = 60 min.
Answer60 minutes
Q11 · CAT 2021 — Anil-Barun-ChanduMedium

Anil in 12 days, Barun in 16 days. Anil, Barun, Chandu together finish in 6 days. Total ₹24,000. Find Chandu's share.

METHODLCM(12,16) = 48. Anil = 4, Barun = 3. In 6 days, Anil = 24, Barun = 18. Total 48. Chandu = 48 − 24 − 18 = 6. Share = 24,000 × 6/48 = 3,000.
Answer₹3,000
Q12 · Workers ratioMedium

Ram completes 60% of a task in 15 days, then takes the help of Rahim (50% as efficient as Ram) and Rachel (50% as efficient as Rahim). In how many more days will they complete the work?

METHODRam's rate = 0.6/15 = 1/25 task/day. Rahim = 1/50. Rachel = 1/100. Combined = 1/25 + 1/50 + 1/100 = 4/100 + 2/100 + 1/100 = 7/100. Remaining work = 0.4. Days = 0.4 × 100/7 = 40/7 ≈ 5.71.
Answer≈ 5.71 days
Q13 · Tank with two pipes opened laterMedium

A tank is filled by pipe A in 10 minutes. Pipe B (drain) is opened after 4 minutes from start. The tank still fills in 16 minutes total. How long does pipe B alone take to drain?

SETUPPipe A operates 16 min. Pipe B operates 12 min. Net = 1 (tank fill).
16 × (1/10) − 12 × (1/b) = 1 ⟹ 1.6 − 12/b = 1 ⟹ 12/b = 0.6 ⟹ b = 20 min.
Answer20 minutes
Q14 · Alternate daysMedium

A can do a task in 6 days and B in 8 days. They work on alternate days starting with A. How long do they take?

METHODLCM(6,8) = 24. A = 4, B = 3. In a 2-day pair: 4 + 3 = 7 units. Six pairs (12 days): 42 units. Need 24, so partial: after 12 days, 42 units done — wait, that exceeds 24. So we finish earlier. Try: after 6 pairs would do 42 (exceeds 24). After 3 pairs (6 days): 21 units. Day 7 (A's turn): 4 more = 25 (exceeds). So in day 7, only need 3/4 of a day. Total = 6 + 3/4 = 6.75 days.

Q20 · Modified efficiencyHard

A and B together finish a task in 12 days. If A worked half as efficiently as usual and B worked thrice as efficiently as usual, the task would take 9 days. How long does A alone take?

Q22 · CAT 2024 — Amal-Vimal-SunilElite

Amal+Vimal: 150 days. Vimal+Sunil: 100 days. Amal alone 75 days, then Vimal alone 135 days, then Sunil alone 45 days completes the task. If Amal works every day, Vimal every 2nd day, Sunil every 3rd day, find total days.

PHASE 1 SOLVE FOR INDIVIDUAL RATESLet a, v, s be the individual rates. a + v = 1/150, v + s = 1/100.
From the sequential: 75a + 135v + 45s = 1.
SOLVEFrom first: v = 1/150 − a. From second: s = 1/100 − v = 1/100 − 1/150 + a = (3 − 2)/300 + a = 1/300 + a.
Sub into 75a + 135v + 45s = 1:
75a + 135(1/150 − a) + 45(1/300 + a) = 1
75a + 0.9 − 135a + 0.15 + 45a = 1
(75 − 135 + 45)a + 1.05 = 1
−15a = −0.05 ⟹ a = 1/300.
So v = 1/150 − 1/300 = 1/300. s = 1/300 + 1/300 = 2/300 = 1/150.
NEW SCHEDULEIn a 6-day cycle (LCM of 1, 2, 3 schedule patterns over the day): Amal works 6 days, Vimal works 3 days, Sunil works 2 days. Work per cycle = 6(1/300) + 3(1/300) + 2(1/150) = 6/300 + 3/300 + 4/300 = 13/300.
Number of cycles = 300/13. Each cycle = 6 days. Total days = 6 × 300/13 = 1800/13 ≈ 138.46 days. Realistically the problem expects a clean number — let me recompute: with rates 1/300, 1/300, 1/150, in days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 the active workers each day are: D1 A+V+S, D2 A only, D3 A+V, D4 A+S, D5 A+V, D6 A+S — actually this depends on interpretation of "every second day", "every third day". For simpler reading: with all rates known, daily computation gives an integer answer.
AnswerApproximately ≈ 138 days (interpretation-dependent)
Q23 · Pipes systemElite

A cistern of 40 litres has an inlet and an outlet. Both opened, fills in 8 minutes. If outflow rate is increased 1.5×, the cistern never fills. Which outflow rate is possible?

SETUPInlet rate − outlet rate = 40/8 = 5 L/min. So inlet − outlet = 5. For 1.5× outlet, inlet ≤ 1.5 × outlet. So outlet ≥ inlet/1.5. Combined with inlet = outlet + 5: outlet ≥ (outlet+5)/1.5 ⟹ 1.5 outlet ≥ outlet + 5 ⟹ 0.5 outlet ≥ 5 ⟹ outlet ≥ 10 L/min.
AnswerOutflow rate ≥ 10 L/min
Q24 · Diophantine fill-drainElite

A fill pipe takes 20 hours, drain pipe takes 30 hours. n pipes (mix of fill and drain) fill the tank in 5 hours. Find a possible value of n.

SETUPLet f fill pipes and d drain pipes, n = f + d. Net rate: f/20 − d/30 = 1/5. Multiply by 60: 3f − 2d = 12 ⟹ 3f = 12 + 2d ⟹ f = 4 + 2d/3. So d must be a multiple of 3.
VALUESd = 0: f = 4, n = 4. d = 3: f = 6, n = 9. d = 6: f = 8, n = 14. d = 9: f = 10, n = 19. So n ∈ {4, 9, 14, 19, …} of form 5k + 4 (or equivalently 5k − 1).
AnswerPossible n: 4, 9, 14, 19, …
Q25 · Capstone — multi-phaseElite

X, Y, Z complete a piece of work individually in 6, 8, 12 hours respectively. X starts at 8 AM. Y joins at 9 AM. Z joins at 10 AM. At what time is the work completed?

LCMW = 24. Rates: X = 4, Y = 3, Z = 2.
PHASES8–9 AM (X alone, 1 h): 4 units. 9–10 AM (X+Y, 1 h): 7 units. From 10 AM (all three): rate 9 units/hr. Remaining = 24 − 4 − 7 = 13 units. Time = 13/9 ≈ 1.444 h = 1 h 26.67 min.
Completion = 10 AM + 1 h 27 min ≈ 11:27 AM.
Answer≈ 11:27 AM

Everything in this codex distilled.

9.1The triangle

The Work Identity Work = Rate × Time
Rate = 1/(individual completion time).
Combined rate = Σ individual rates.

9.2The LCM trick

LCM Method Total work W = LCM of individual times.
Each worker's units/day = W / individual time.
Combined time = W / (sum of units/day).

9.3Two-worker formula

Quick Formula Together time = nm/(n+m)
For just two workers. Use LCM for three or more.

9.4Wages

Wage Distribution Each worker's share = total wages × (units of work done by that worker) / (total units of work).

9.5Pipes & cisterns

Pipes Net rate = Σ inlet rates − Σ outlet rates.
Fill time = capacity / net rate (if positive).
If net ≤ 0, tank never fills.

9.6Standard archetypes

  • Two workers together: nm/(n+m).
  • Phased work (one joins late): track work per phase, sum to W.
  • Wage distribution: proportional to work done, not days.
  • Pipes with leak: leak is a drain pipe; compute from slowdown.
  • Garrison / ration: total food = soldiers × days; rebalance after changes.

9.7The traps catalogue

  • Wages by days, not work.
  • Outlet pipe sign forgotten.
  • Hours-per-day mismatch.
  • Mixing fractions when LCM trick would be cleaner.
  • Misreading efficiency direction.

9.8Recommended drill sequence

  1. Day 1: The triangle, basic two-worker problems.
  2. Day 2: LCM trick — three or more workers.
  3. Day 3: Phased work — joins and leaves.
  4. Day 4: Wage distribution.
  5. Day 5: Pipes and cisterns.
  6. Day 6: Problem bank, all tiers.
The work mantra. Take LCM. Convert to integer units. Track phases. Sum to total. Every CAT time-and-work problem yields to this discipline. The verbal complexity is intentional disguise; the underlying math is one identity, applied cleanly.
