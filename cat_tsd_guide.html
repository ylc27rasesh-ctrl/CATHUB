Module 00 · Orientation

The most-tested
arithmetic chapter
in CAT.

A teacher's overview. Time, Speed and Distance produces three to four CAT questions every year — more than any other arithmetic topic. The technique surface is wider than most chapters, but the underlying mental model is just one triangle.

TSD is the chapter that punishes weak fundamentals more than any other in the arithmetic block. The formula distance = speed × time is grade-school level. But CAT setters layer the formula with relative motion, lengthy trains, downstream-upstream complications, circular tracks, and meeting-time twists — each producing recognizable archetypes that only fluency unlocks. Strong TSD students are almost always strong CAT students. Weak TSD students rarely break the 95th percentile.

The technique surface includes: basic triangle (Module 1), relative speed (Module 2), trains and crossings (Module 3), boats and streams (Module 4), circular tracks (Module 5), and average and variable speed (Module 6). Six modules, each a self-contained sub-topic. Drill them in order.

0.1How to use this codex in class

The nine modules are sequenced for an 8–10 hour block. Module 1 is the foundational triangle. Modules 2 through 6 cover the five major application contexts. Each is partially independent — once Module 1 is solid, a teacher can dip into any later module by topic. Module 7 collects tactics, Module 8 is the question bank, Module 9 is the wall poster.

Teaching philosophy. Every TSD problem is a triangle: speed × time = distance. Three quantities, three equations, one unknown. Reveal the triangle in every problem you teach. The complexity of CAT TSD problems comes from which triangle is being asked about (the train? the man? the relative motion?) — never from new physics. Identify the right triangle, write its three sides, solve.

0.2What the CAT actually asks

  • Direct distance/speed/time computation. Find the third given the other two. Five seconds.
  • Two travelers meeting head-on. Relative speed addition. CAT 2024 Anil-Sunil at 45 km is this archetype.
  • Two travelers chasing. Relative speed subtraction.
  • Trains crossing each other or a pole or a platform. Length matters. Crossing-time problems are mechanical once the framework is in place.
  • Boats in still water vs streams. Downstream = boat + stream; upstream = boat − stream. CAT 2024 had two boats with constant water flow.
  • Circular tracks. Meeting points, first meetings, when do they meet again at the start?
  • Average speed (with equal distances). Harmonic mean — already met in Volume II.
  • Bus/train/man arriving early or late. Two-scenario problems with shared distance.
  • Meeting-then-completing problems. "After meeting, A takes a hours, B takes b hours." The √(a/b) speed-ratio trick.

Classroom diagnostic in two minutes. Ask: "A train 200 m long, running at 72 km/h, crosses a man walking at 18 km/h in the same direction. How long does it take?" The fluent student converts speeds to m/s (72 → 20, 18 → 5), computes relative speed (20 − 5 = 15), divides length by relative speed (200/15 ≈ 13.33 s). Forty seconds. The struggling student leaves units in km/h, gets entangled. Use the diagnostic to gauge module-2 readiness.

0.3Unit conversions — the silent killer

Most TSD errors come from mismatched units. Train length in metres, speed in km/h, time in seconds. Convert everything to a consistent system before computing. The most useful conversion to memorise:

The Conversion Identity 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s
1 m/s = 18/5 km/h = 3.6 km/h

So 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s.

Train problems live almost entirely in metres and seconds. Long-distance problems live in km and hours. Convert at the start; never midway.

Module 01 · The Triangle

The triangle
of motion.

Three quantities — speed, time, distance — connected by one identity. Every TSD problem in the entire chapter is an exercise in choosing which side of the triangle to compute.

1.1The core identity

The Triangle Distance = Speed × Time
Equivalently: Speed = Distance / Time
And: Time = Distance / Speed

Three quantities, three rearrangements. One identity rules them all.

1.2The two variation patterns hidden in the triangle

From Volume V's variation framework:

For a fixed distance

Speed and time are inversely proportional. Double the speed, halve the time. Speed × Time = Distance = constant.

For a fixed time

Speed and distance are directly proportional. Double the speed, double the distance. Distance = Speed × Time where Time is constant.

1.3The "scenario-comparison" archetype

Many CAT TSD problems describe two scenarios with the same distance. Use inverse proportion of speed and time to set up one equation.

Foundation example — late vs early Easy

A man walks at 12 km/h and reaches the railway station 10 minutes after the train has departed. If he had walked at 15 km/h, he would have reached 10 minutes before the train's departure. Find the distance from his home to the station.

STEP 1 · Frame Let distance = d km. Time at 12 km/h = d/12 hours. Time at 15 km/h = d/15 hours.
Difference in times = (10 min late) − (−10 min early) = 20 minutes = 1/3 hour.
STEP 2 · Equation d/12 − d/15 = 1/3
(5d − 4d)/60 = 1/3 ⟹ d/60 = 1/3 ⟹ d = 20 km.
AnswerDistance = 20 km

1.4The CAT 2024 train-trouble problem

CAT 2024 — train speed and time saved Medium

A train traveled a certain distance at a uniform speed. Had the speed been 6 km/h more, it would have needed 4 hours less. Had the speed been 6 km/h less, it would have needed 6 hours more. Find the distance.

STEP 1 · Set up Let original speed = s, time = t. Distance d = st.
At s + 6: d = (s + 6)(t − 4) = st − 4s + 6t − 24 ⟹ 4s − 6t + 24 = 0 ⟹ 2s − 3t + 12 = 0.
At s − 6: d = (s − 6)(t + 6) = st + 6s − 6t − 36 ⟹ 6s − 6t − 36 = 0 ⟹ s − t − 6 = 0 ⟹ s = t + 6.
STEP 2 · Solve Substitute s = t + 6 into 2s − 3t + 12 = 0: 2(t + 6) − 3t + 12 = 0 ⟹ −t + 24 = 0 ⟹ t = 24. So s = 30.
Distance = 30 × 24 = 720 km.
Answer720 km
· In-class drill ·

Mental math, ten seconds each. Find the third quantity:

240 km in 4 h: speed? · 72 km/h for 2.5 h: distance? · 540 km at 60 km/h: time? · 54 km/h = ? m/s · 30 m/s = ? km/h

Answers: 60 km/h, 180 km, 9 hours, 15 m/s, 108 km/h.

Module 02 · Relative Motion

Relative
speed.

When two objects move simultaneously, their relative speed determines when they meet or how long one takes to catch the other. Two rules — same direction subtracts, opposite direction adds — and you handle most TSD problems.

2.1The two cases

Relative Speed Two objects moving in opposite directions:
Relative speed = s₁ + s₂

Two objects moving in the same direction:
Relative speed = |s₁ − s₂|

The intuition is mechanical. If you and your friend are walking toward each other, you close the gap fast — your speeds add. If she's walking ahead of you in the same direction, you only close the gap by the difference in your speeds.

2.2Time to meet — head-on case

Two travelers start from points A and B (distance d apart) moving toward each other at speeds s₁ and s₂. Time to meet:

Head-on meeting Time to meet = d / (s₁ + s₂)

Worked: A and B are 60 km apart, walking toward each other at 4 and 6 km/h. They meet after 60/(4+6) = 6 hours.

2.3Time to catch up — chasing case

Chasing Time to catch up = gap / (s_faster − s_slower)

If A is 12 km ahead of B, and A walks at 3 km/h while B walks at 5 km/h, B catches up in 12/(5−3) = 6 hours.

2.4The CAT 2024 Anil-Sunil problem

CAT 2024 — Anil and Sunil cross Hard

Two places A and B are 45 km apart and connected by a straight road. Anil goes from A to B while Sunil goes from B to A. Starting at the same time, they cross each other in exactly 1 hour 30 minutes. If Anil reaches B exactly 1 hour 15 minutes after Sunil reaches A, find Anil's speed in km/h.

STEP 1 · Set up variables Let Anil's speed = a km/h; Sunil's speed = s km/h.
They meet after 1.5 h: a + s = 45/1.5 = 30 ⟹ a + s = 30.
STEP 2 · Times taken Anil's total time = 45/a. Sunil's total time = 45/s.
Anil reaches B 1.25 h after Sunil reaches A: 45/a − 45/s = 1.25.
STEP 3 · Solve From step 1: s = 30 − a. Substitute: 45/a − 45/(30 − a) = 1.25.
45[(30 − a) − a] / [a(30 − a)] = 1.25
45(30 − 2a) = 1.25 × a(30 − a)
(30 − 2a) × 45 = 1.25a(30 − a)
1350 − 90a = 37.5a − 1.25a²
1.25a² − 127.5a + 1350 = 0
a² − 102a + 1080 = 0
a = (102 ± √(10404 − 4320))/2 = (102 ± √6084)/2 = (102 ± 78)/2.
So a = 90 (impossible, since then s < 0) or a = 12.
AnswerAnil's speed = 12 km/h

2.5The "meeting-and-completing" trick

A famous structural shortcut: two travelers start from opposite ends, meet somewhere in between, and continue to their destinations. If after meeting, A takes a hours and B takes b hours, the speed ratio satisfies:

Meet-and-Complete Identity Speed_A / Speed_B = √(b/a)
Where a is A's time after meeting and b is B's time after meeting.

This identity comes from the fact that the distance each covered after the meeting equals what the other covered before the meeting (because they meet at the same point).

Meet-and-Complete — Arvind and Surbhi Hard

Arvind travels from town A to town B, and Surbhi from town B to town A, starting simultaneously along the same route. After meeting, Arvind takes 6 hours to reach B while Surbhi takes 24 hours to reach A. If Arvind's speed is 54 km/h, find the distance between A and B.

STEP 1 · Speed ratio Arvind/Surbhi = √(24/6) = √4 = 2. So Surbhi = 27 km/h.
STEP 2 · Time to meeting Let total distance = D and time to meet = t. Distance Arvind covered before meeting = 54t; this equals distance Surbhi covers after meeting in 24 hours = 27 × 24 = 648.
So 54t = 648 ⟹ t = 12 hours.
STEP 3 · Total distance D = (54 + 27) × 12 = 81 × 12 = 972 km.
AnswerDistance = 972 km

Module 03 · Trains

Trains, length,
and crossings.

When the moving object has length itself. The total distance to cover during a "crossing" is the sum of lengths of all objects involved. This single insight unlocks the entire train-problem family.

3.1The four crossing scenarios

Train problems vary by what the train is crossing — and what counts as "distance covered":

Train Crossing Formulas Train of length L crossing a stationary point (pole, man standing): distance = L
Train of length L crossing a platform of length P: distance = L + P
Train of length L₁ crossing another train of length L₂: distance = L₁ + L₂

In each case: Time = distance / relative speed

3.2Two trains — opposite vs same direction

Two trains crossing each other can move in opposite or same direction. Their relative speed is computed accordingly:

  • Opposite directions: relative speed = s₁ + s₂. Crossing time = (L₁ + L₂) / (s₁ + s₂).
  • Same direction: relative speed = |s₁ − s₂|. Crossing time = (L₁ + L₂) / |s₁ − s₂|.

3.3Worked example — fundamental

Train crossing a platform Easy

A train 200 m long, running at 72 km/h, crosses a platform 300 m long. Find the crossing time.

CONVERT 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s.
COMPUTE Total distance = 200 + 300 = 500 m. Time = 500/20 = 25 s.
Answer25 seconds

3.4The CAT 2017 trains-meeting problem

CAT 2017 — trains A and B meeting Hard

Trains A and B start traveling at the same time towards each other with constant speeds from stations X and Y, respectively. Train A reaches station Y in 10 minutes while train B takes 9 minutes to reach station X after meeting train A. Find the total time, in minutes, taken by train B to travel from Y to X.

SETUP Let t = time to meet. After meeting, A takes (10 − t) min to reach Y; B takes 9 min to reach X.
APPLY THE MEET-COMPLETE IDENTITY t² = (10 − t) × 9 ⟹ t² + 9t − 90 = 0 ⟹ (t + 15)(t − 6) = 0 ⟹ t = 6.
TOTAL TIME FOR B B takes t + 9 = 6 + 9 = 15 minutes.
Answer15 minutes

3.5The two-stage crossing — front-end vs rear-end

Some CAT problems ask about partial crossings: "the front of train A crossed the rear of train B X seconds before…". The trick is to recognize that the front-to-rear crossing covers a distance equal to the length of one train, while the full crossing covers both lengths.

Module 04 · Boats & Streams

Boats and
streams.

When the medium itself moves. Downstream = boat + stream. Upstream = boat − stream. Two equations, two unknowns. Every CAT boat problem is a two-line solve once the frame is in place.

4.1The framework

Boat in Stream — Two Speeds Let b = boat speed in still water, s = stream speed.
Downstream speed = b + s
Upstream speed = b − s

Equivalently:
b = (down + up)/2
s = (down − up)/2

4.2The standard problem

Boat — direct still-water speed Easy

A boat covers 20 km downstream in 2 hours and 16 km upstream in 4 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water and the stream.

METHOD Downstream = 20/2 = 10 km/h. Upstream = 16/4 = 4 km/h.
Boat in still water = (10 + 4)/2 = 7 km/h. Stream = (10 − 4)/2 = 3 km/h.
AnswerBoat = 7 km/h; Stream = 3 km/h

4.3The CAT 2024 two-boat problem

CAT 2024 — Two boats with constant river Elite

A boat takes 2 hours to travel downstream from port A to port B, and 3 hours to return to port A. Another boat takes a total of 6 hours to travel from B to A and return to B. If the speeds of the boats and the river are constant, find the time taken by the slower boat to travel from A to B.

STEP 1 · First boat Let d = distance AB, r = stream speed.
d/(b₁ + r) = 2 and d/(b₁ − r) = 3.
So b₁ + r = d/2 and b₁ − r = d/3. Adding: 2b₁ = 5d/6 ⟹ b₁ = 5d/12. Subtracting: 2r = d/6 ⟹ r = d/12.
STEP 2 · Second boat Total time = d/(b₂ + r) + d/(b₂ − r) = 6.
Substituting r = d/12: d/(b₂ + d/12) + d/(b₂ − d/12) = 6.
Let x = 12b₂/d. Then: 12/(x + 1) + 12/(x − 1) = 6 ⟹ 2/(x + 1) + 2/(x − 1) = 1 ⟹ [2(x − 1) + 2(x + 1)]/[(x+1)(x−1)] = 1 ⟹ 4x/(x² − 1) = 1 ⟹ x² − 4x − 1 = 0 ⟹ x = (4 ± √20)/2 = 2 ± √5.
Take x = 2 + √5 ≈ 4.236 (must be positive). So 12b₂/d = 2 + √5 ⟹ b₂ = d(2 + √5)/12.
STEP 3 · Identify slower b₁ = 5d/12 ≈ 0.417d. b₂ ≈ d × 4.236/12 ≈ 0.353d. So boat 2 is slower.
Slower boat A → B time = d/(b₂ + r) = d / (d(2 + √5)/12 + d/12) = 12/(3 + √5).
Rationalize: 12(3 − √5)/((3+√5)(3−√5)) = 12(3 − √5)/(9 − 5) = 3(3 − √5) = 9 − 3√5 ≈ 9 − 6.71 ≈ 2.29 hours.
Answer≈ 9 − 3√5 hours (≈ 2.29 h)

4.4The "current makes no difference" insight

If a boat travels a round trip (downstream then upstream over the same distance), the stream's contribution doesn't cancel — average speed is the harmonic mean of downstream and upstream speeds. This is the same identity from Volume II's average-speed module.

Module 05 · Circular Motion

Circular tracks
and races.

When the path closes on itself. New questions become possible — "when do they meet again at the start?" — but the relative-speed engine still runs everything.

5.1Meeting on a circular track

Two runners on a circular track of length L starting at the same point:

First Meeting on a Circular Track Opposite directions: First meeting after time L / (s₁ + s₂)
Same direction: First meeting after time L / |s₁ − s₂| (faster laps the slower by one full loop)

5.2Number of meeting points

Over many laps, where do the two runners meet? This depends on the ratio of their speeds.

Meeting Points on a Circular Track If speed ratio is a : b in lowest form:
Opposite directions: a + b distinct meeting points.
Same direction: |a − b| distinct meeting points.

5.3When do they meet at the starting point?

Each runner returns to the starting point after their own lap time. They simultaneously return to the start at the LCM of their lap times.

First Meeting at Start Lap time of runner i = L/sᵢ.
First meeting at start = LCM(L/s₁, L/s₂)

5.4Worked example

Circular track — meeting Medium

Two runners A and B start running on a circular track of length 360 m from the same point at the same time. A runs at 6 m/s and B at 4 m/s. If they run in opposite directions, when do they first meet? When do they first meet again at the starting point?

FIRST MEETING Opposite directions, relative speed = 10 m/s. First meeting after 360/10 = 36 s.
MEETING AT START Lap times: A = 360/6 = 60 s, B = 360/4 = 90 s. LCM = 180 s = 3 minutes.
AnswerFirst meeting: 36 s; at start: 180 s

5.5Races and head-starts

"A gives B a head start of x metres in a race of distance d." This is a CAT variant of relative speed — A starts behind by x and must close the gap before the finish line.

The condition "A beats B by x metres" means A finishes the race while B is x metres short. Useful identity: in a race of length d, if A beats B by x metres, then A's distance is d when B's is d − x. The speed ratio: s_A / s_B = d / (d − x).

Module 06 · Average & Variable

Average speed
and variable
motion.

When the speed changes during the journey. The wrong instinct is to average the speeds; the right one is to compute total distance over total time. The harmonic mean returns.

6.1The definition

Average Speed Average speed = Total distance / Total time

Never the average of speeds, unless the times are equal.

6.2The equal-distance shortcut — harmonic mean

If a body covers two equal distances at speeds u and v, the average speed is:

Equal Distances — Harmonic Mean Avg speed = 2uv / (u + v)

Always less than the arithmetic mean. The slower leg takes more time, dragging the average down.

Worked: 100 km at 40 km/h and 100 km at 60 km/h. Harmonic mean = 2 × 40 × 60 / (40 + 60) = 4800/100 = 48 km/h. Not 50.

6.3Three or more legs

For three equal distances at speeds u, v, w: average = 3/(1/u + 1/v + 1/w). The harmonic mean of n speeds.

6.4The CAT 2024 bus scheduling problem

CAT 2024 — bus scheduling Elite

A bus starts at 9 am and follows a fixed route every day. One day, it traveled at a constant speed of 60 km/h and reached its destination 3.5 hours later than its scheduled arrival time. The next day, it traveled two-thirds of its route in one-third of its total scheduled travel time, and the remaining part of the route at 40 km/h to reach just on time. Find the scheduled arrival time.

STEP 1 · Set up variables Let D = total distance, T = scheduled travel time. Scheduled speed = D/T.
Day 1: 60 km/h, time = D/60, which is T + 3.5. So D/60 = T + 3.5 ⟹ D = 60T + 210.
STEP 2 · Day 2 conditions First leg: 2D/3 covered in T/3.
Second leg: D/3 at 40 km/h, takes D/120.
Total time = T/3 + D/120 = T (just on time). So D/120 = 2T/3 ⟹ D = 80T.
STEP 3 · Solve 80T = 60T + 210 ⟹ 20T = 210 ⟹ T = 10.5 hours.
Bus starts 9 am; arrives at 9 am + 10.5 h = 7:30 pm.
AnswerScheduled arrival = 7:30 pm

Module 07 · Tactics

Twelve tips,
tricks, and traps.

The accumulated wisdom from teaching TSD — what to drill, what to whisper, and the recurring mistakes that wreck students under time pressure.

TIP 01 · UNITS
Convert first, compute later

Train problems: m and s. Long-distance: km and h. Mixing units within a single computation is the most common error in this chapter.

TIP 02 · 5/18
Memorise the conversion factor

km/h × 5/18 = m/s. m/s × 18/5 = km/h. Drill until automatic.

TIP 03 · TRIANGLE
Identify the triangle first

What's the distance, what's the speed, what's the time? Write all three on scratch paper before equations.

TIP 04 · RELATIVE
Same direction subtracts, opposite adds

The basic rule of relative motion. Read the problem twice to confirm direction.

TIP 05 · LENGTH
Train crossings include both lengths

Train + platform: L + P. Train + train: L₁ + L₂. Train + pole: just L. Get the total distance right.

TIP 06 · √RATIO
Meet-complete identity: speeds in √ ratio of times

If A takes a hours after meeting and B takes b, then s_A / s_B = √(b/a). Memorise.

TIP 07 · BOATS
(down + up)/2 and (down − up)/2

Boat speed = average. Stream speed = half difference. Always.

TIP 08 · CIRCULAR
LCM of lap times for start-meetings

For meeting at the starting point: LCM of individual lap times. For first meeting anywhere: relative-speed formula.

TIP 09 · AVERAGE
Harmonic mean for equal distances

2uv/(u+v) for equal-distance round trips. Never the arithmetic mean.

TIP 10 · TIME GAPS
For "x min late vs y min early" problems

Time difference = (x + y) minutes. Convert to hours and solve d/v₁ − d/v₂ = (x + y)/60.

TIP 11 · RATIOS
For fixed distance, time ∝ 1/speed

Speed ratio 3 : 4 means time ratio 4 : 3 for the same distance. Use this to skip computation.

TIP 12 · SANITY
Plug back into the scenario

After solving, verify each piece of info in the problem. Often catches sign errors.

7.1Five traps

Trap 1 — Mixing units. Speeds in km/h, distances in m, times in s. Always pick one system and convert everything before computing.

Trap 2 — Averaging speeds. "60 km/h going, 40 km/h returning" — the average is NOT 50. It's the harmonic mean = 48.

Trap 3 — Forgetting train length. "Train crosses a man in 20 seconds" — distance is the train length, not zero. Easy to overlook in haste.

Trap 4 — Wrong direction for relative speed. Re-read whether trains/boats are going in the same or opposite direction. Adding when you should subtract halves the answer.

Trap 5 — Boat in still water vs downstream. The boat's "speed" in problem statements is usually still-water speed unless otherwise stated. Don't confuse it with downstream speed.

7.2The two-minute checklist

  1. Read twice. Identify what's moving and in which direction.
  2. Note all given quantities with units.
  3. Convert to a single unit system (m/s and m, or km/h and km).
  4. Identify the triangle: which is distance, which is speed, which is time?
  5. If two objects: compute relative speed.
  6. If trains: include both lengths in distance.
  7. Set up the equation; solve.
  8. Plug back to verify.

Q17 · CAT 2017 — trains meetingHard

Trains A and B from X and Y move toward each other. After meeting, A takes 10 − t min to reach Y; B takes 9 min to reach X. Find B's total time Y → X.

METHODMeet-complete: t² = 9(10−t) ⟹ t² + 9t − 90 = 0 ⟹ t = 6. B's total = 6 + 9 = 15 min.

Q18 · Boat doubling trickHard

A boat travels downstream to office and back. If the boat's speed is doubled (river unchanged), the total time is reduced by 75%. Find the ratio of the original boat speed to river speed.

SETUPLet b = boat, r = river, d = distance. Original time T = d/(b+r) + d/(b−r) = d × 2b/(b²−r²).
New time with 2b: T' = d × 2(2b)/((2b)²−r²) = 4bd/(4b²−r²).
T' = T/4: 4bd/(4b²−r²) = (1/4) × 2bd/(b²−r²) = bd/(2(b²−r²)).
SOLVECross-multiply: 8(b² − r²) = 4b² − r² ⟹ 4b² = 7r² ⟹ b² = 7r²/4 ⟹ b/r = √7/2.
So ratio = √7 : 2.
Answer√7 : 2

Q22 · CAT 2024 — train speed +6/−6Elite

A train traveled a certain distance at uniform speed. At +6 km/h, 4 hours less; at −6 km/h, 6 hours more. Find distance.

METHOD(s+6)(t−4) = st ⟹ 6t − 4s = 24. (s−6)(t+6) = st ⟹ 6s − 6t = 36 ⟹ s − t = 6. From the first: 3t − 2s = 12. Substituting s = t + 6: 3t − 2t − 12 = 12 ⟹ t = 24. s = 30. d = 720 km.

Q24 · CAT 2024 — two boats slowerElite

Boat 1: A→B downstream 2 h, B→A upstream 3 h. Boat 2: A→B and back total 6 h. Find time for slower boat A→B.

9.1Core identity

The Triangle D = S × T · S = D/T · T = D/S

9.2Conversions

Conversions km/h → m/s : ×5/18
m/s → km/h : ×18/5

9.3Relative speed

Relative Motion Same direction: |s₁ − s₂|
Opposite directions: s₁ + s₂

9.4Train crossings

  • Train crossing pole: distance = L (train length)
  • Train crossing platform: distance = L + P
  • Two trains crossing each other: distance = L₁ + L₂

9.5Boats & streams

Boat–Stream Downstream = b + s · Upstream = b − s
b = (down + up)/2 · s = (down − up)/2

9.6Meet-and-complete identity

After-Meeting Times s_A / s_B = √(t_B / t_A)
where t_X is X's time after meeting.

9.7Circular track

  • First meeting (opposite): L/(s₁+s₂)
  • First meeting (same dir): L/|s₁−s₂|
  • Meeting at start: LCM of lap times
  • Distinct meeting points: a+b (opp) or |a−b| (same), where speed ratio is a:b

9.8Average speed

Average Speed Avg = Total distance / Total time
For equal distances: harmonic mean = 2uv/(u+v)

9.9The traps catalogue

  • Always convert units first.
  • Don't average speeds — average distances / total times.
  • Train problems include train length.
  • Read direction carefully — same vs opposite.
  • Boat speed in stream ≠ boat speed in still water.
