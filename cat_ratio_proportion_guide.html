Module 00 · Orientation

The language
of relationships
between quantities.

A teacher's overview. Ratio and proportion is the grammar of comparison. It runs underneath mixtures, partnership problems, ages, work-rate, and half of geometry. Teach it well and a dozen later chapters become easier.

Ratio is the most under-appreciated topic in the CAT syllabus. It generates only two to four direct questions per CAT, but it is the silent prerequisite for mixtures (Vol. IV), partnership profit-sharing, work-rate (Vol. VII), speed-time-distance (Vol. VI), and geometric similarity. A student fluent in ratios moves through the arithmetic block effortlessly; a student weak in ratios stumbles in every chapter that follows.

The good news: there are exactly four core ideas. The notion of a ratio as a multiplier of an unknown. The notion of proportion as equal ratios. The notion of variation as functional relationship. And the technique of combining two-quantity ratios into multi-quantity ratios. Master these four, and the entire chapter unfolds.

0.1How to use this codex in class

The nine modules are sequenced for a 6–8 hour block. Module 1 establishes the most important reframe in the entire chapter — a ratio is not just a : b, it is the assertion that some unknown k exists such that quantities are ak and bk. Once a student writes things this way, ratio problems become algebra problems with a single variable. Module 2 covers the standard operations (combining, splitting, scaling). Module 3 covers proportion and its classical properties. Module 4 covers variation, which is functional thinking dressed in arithmetic clothes. Module 5 tackles multi-quantity ratio compounding — the hardest technique in this chapter.

Teaching philosophy. The unifying claim of this volume: every ratio is a multiplier waiting for its multiplicand. Write 3 : 4 as 3k : 4k. Now you have algebra. Now solve. This single move converts CAT 2023 (coin collectors), CAT 2024 (fruit-seller's 187 fruits), and CAT 2024 (Rajesh-Garima ages) into one-line problems.

0.2What the CAT actually asks

From the 2017–2025 CAT papers, the recurring archetypes are:

  • Two-quantity ratio with a condition. "A : B = 3 : 4, and A − B = 25; find A". Use the multiplier method, one line.
  • Ratio changes after an event. "Original ratio is 3 : 4; A spends some, ratio becomes 2 : 3; find original amount". Two unknowns, one event, two equations.
  • Multi-quantity ratios. "A : B = 2 : 3, B : C = 4 : 5; find A : B : C". The compounding technique from Module 5.
  • Ages and time. "Five years ago their ages were in ratio 3 : 4; five years from now they will be in ratio 5 : 6; find their present ages". Two equations, two unknowns.
  • Direct variation. "Y varies directly as X²; when X = 2, Y = 12; find Y when X = 5". Substitute and solve.
  • Inverse variation. Number of workers and time, speed and time, pressure and volume. Product is constant.
  • Joint variation. Volume varies as length × breadth × height. Compound the variations.
  • Partition / partnership problems. Splitting a profit or a total in a given ratio. Add the parts, divide.
  • Integer-constraint ratio problems. "Three numbers in ratio 3 : 4 : 5, all integers, total under 100; how many such sets exist?". The CAT 2023 coin-collector problem is this archetype.

Classroom diagnostic in two minutes. Ask: "The ratio of A's salary to B's salary is 5 : 7. If A's salary increases by Rs. 4000 and B's by Rs. 6000, the new ratio is 7 : 10. What was A's original salary?" The fluent student writes 5k and 7k, sets up (5k + 4000)/(7k + 6000) = 7/10, solves k = 2000, gets A = 10,000. Forty-five seconds. The struggling student starts assigning two separate variables and gets tangled in cross-multiplication. Use this diagnostic to identify how much Module 1 needs to be drilled.

0.3The vocabulary

  • Ratio. A comparison of two quantities of the same kind, written a : b. Means a/b. Order matters.
  • Antecedent and consequent. In a : b, a is the antecedent, b the consequent. Rarely used in CAT questions but appears occasionally.
  • Proportion. An equality of two ratios: a : b = c : d. Equivalent to ad = bc.
  • Means and extremes. In a : b = c : d, the means are b and c (middle terms); the extremes are a and d (outer terms). Product of means = product of extremes.
  • Direct variation. Y = kX. When X doubles, Y doubles.
  • Inverse variation. Y = k/X. When X doubles, Y halves.
  • Joint variation. Y depends on more than one variable, multiplicatively. E.g. Y = kXZ or Y = kX²/Z.

Module 01 · The Frame

A ratio is
a multiplier
in disguise.

The single reframing that makes this entire chapter easy. Stop reading a : b as a comparison. Start reading it as the algebraic assertion that quantities equal ak and bk for some k. Then solve for k.

1.1The definition

A ratio of two quantities A and B of the same kind is written A : B and means A/B. The colon is just notation for division. So "milk to water in ratio 3 : 1" means milk/water = 3, equivalently milk = 3 × water.

Three subtle features worth noting on day one:

  • Same kind. A ratio compares quantities of the same kind — litres to litres, rupees to rupees, kilograms to kilograms. Comparing rupees to kilograms is not a ratio; it's a rate (we'll meet rates in variation, Module 4).
  • Order matters. A : B and B : A are not the same. 3 : 4 is not 4 : 3.
  • Scale invariant. Multiplying both sides by the same number doesn't change the ratio. 3 : 4 = 6 : 8 = 9 : 12 = 30 : 40. This is the key to the entire chapter.

1.2The k-multiplier framing — the key move

When a problem says "the ratio of A to B is 3 : 4", you should not write "A = 3, B = 4". That makes A and B specific numbers, which they almost never are. Instead, write:

The k-Multiplier Framing "A : B = 3 : 4" means there exists some unknown k > 0 such that
A = 3k and B = 4k

The number k is what you solve for.

The number k captures the actual scale. If we later learn A − B = 10, then 3k − 4k = −k = something, no wait — let's check direction: actually let's try a different example. If A − B = 5 and A : B = 7 : 4, then 7k − 4k = 3k = 5, so k = 5/3, giving A = 35/3, B = 20/3. The point is: one ratio plus one additional equation pins down the actual quantities.

1.3Why this beats the alternative

The alternative is to write A and B as two separate variables — A and B — and solve a system. This works but is slow. The k-multiplier compresses two unknowns into one, because the ratio already tells you the relationship between them. Why carry two variables when one will do?

Slow approach

Let A and B be salaries. Set up A/B = 5/7 and a second equation (e.g. A + B = 12,000). Solve two equations in two unknowns. Cross-multiply, substitute, simplify.

Fast approach

Let A = 5k, B = 7k. Then 5k + 7k = 12k = 12,000, so k = 1,000, A = 5,000, B = 7,000. One variable, one equation, one minute.

1.4Simplifying ratios — the canonical form

The canonical form of a ratio is the one in lowest terms. 12 : 18 simplifies to 2 : 3 (divide both by 6). 25 : 75 to 1 : 3. Always simplify before computing — the smaller numbers are easier to work with.

Fractional ratios should be cleared. 1/2 : 1/3 becomes 3 : 2 (multiply both by 6). 0.4 : 0.5 becomes 4 : 5. 2.5 : 7.5 becomes 1 : 3.

1.5Worked walkthrough — the k-multiplier in action

Foundation example — ratio change after spending Easy

The ratio of A's money to B's money is 5 : 7. A spends Rs. 200 and B spends Rs. 100. The new ratio of their money becomes 1 : 2. How much money did A originally have?

STEP 1 · Frame A = 5k, B = 7k.
STEP 2 · Apply the condition After spending: (5k − 200)/(7k − 100) = 1/2.
Cross-multiply: 2(5k − 200) = 7k − 100 ⟹ 10k − 400 = 7k − 100 ⟹ 3k = 300 ⟹ k = 100.
STEP 3 · Recover A A = 5k = Rs. 500.
AnswerA originally had Rs. 500

Teaching note: the entire problem collapses to a single linear equation in k. Without the k-framing, you'd need two variables and a longer setup.

· In-class drill ·

For each ratio, state the simplest form. Mental math, fifteen seconds each:

20 : 35 · 0.6 : 0.9 · 1/4 : 1/6 · 2.5 : 10 · 121 : 11

Answers: 4:7, 2:3, 3:2, 1:4, 11:1.

Module 02 · Operations

Manipulating
ratios.

The standard moves — adding the parts, dividing a total, combining two ratios into a chain, and the small algebraic identities (componendo, dividendo) that occasionally win seconds in elite problems.

2.1Dividing a total in a ratio — the most common operation

If a total T is divided in the ratio a : b, the parts are:

Partition in Ratio a : b Part 1 = T × a/(a + b)
Part 2 = T × b/(a + b)

For three parts in ratio a : b : c:
Each part = T × (a or b or c)/(a + b + c)

Worked: Rs. 2,400 divided between A and B in ratio 5 : 7. A's share = 2400 × 5/12 = 1000. B's share = 2400 × 7/12 = 1400. Total parts = 12; each part is worth 200.

2.2Scaling and equivalent ratios

Multiplying both terms by the same number preserves the ratio. This lets us match scales when comparing ratios from different sources. If A : B = 2 : 3 and C : D = 4 : 6, both ratios are 2 : 3.

2.3The compounding identity — combining two ratios into a multi-quantity ratio

If A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5, what is A : B : C? Naive students plug in numbers and get confused. The technique: scale both ratios so they share the same number for B.

A : B = 2 : 3. Multiply by 4: A : B = 8 : 12.
B : C = 4 : 5. Multiply by 3: B : C = 12 : 15.
Now B is 12 in both. So A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15.

The Compound Ratio Technique To combine A : B = p : q and B : C = r : s:
Scale to make the B-values match (LCM of q and r).
Result: A : B : C = pr : qr : qs

2.4Adding ratios — when it's valid and when it isn't

Adding ratios is NOT what students think. If A : B = 2 : 3 and C : D = 4 : 5, then (A + C) : (B + D) is NOT 6 : 8 or 2 : 3 generally. It depends on the absolute values, not just the ratios. To add quantities given by ratios, you must first know the actual scale of each.

2.5The classical properties — componendo, dividendo, alternendo

These are old algebraic identities that occasionally simplify elite problems. Worth knowing but rarely worth memorising as named techniques.

Properties of Equal Ratios Given a/b = c/d:
Alternendo: a/c = b/d
Invertendo: b/a = d/c
Componendo: (a + b)/b = (c + d)/d
Dividendo: (a − b)/b = (c − d)/d
Componendo & Dividendo: (a + b)/(a − b) = (c + d)/(c − d)

The componendo-dividendo identity is occasionally useful when CAT gives you (a + b)/(a − b) and asks for a/b or vice versa. Worth recognising on sight.

2.6Worked applications

Compounding two ratios Medium

The ratio of A's age to B's age is 3 : 4. The ratio of B's age to C's age is 5 : 6. If C is 36 years old, how old is A?

STEP 1 · Compound A : B = 3 : 4. B : C = 5 : 6. Scale to match B: multiply first by 5 (giving 15 : 20), second by 4 (giving 20 : 24). So A : B : C = 15 : 20 : 24.
STEP 2 · Use C's age C corresponds to 24 parts. 24 parts = 36 years ⟹ each part = 1.5. A = 15 × 1.5 = 22.5 years.
AnswerA is 22.5 years
Partition with multiple unknowns Medium

A sum of Rs. 5,500 is divided among A, B, and C such that A gets 3/5 of what B gets, and B gets 2/3 of what C gets. Find C's share.

STEP 1 · Build the ratio A = (3/5)B and B = (2/3)C, so A = (3/5)(2/3)C = (2/5)C.
So A : B : C = (2/5)C : (2/3)C : C. Multiply by 15 to clear fractions: 6 : 10 : 15. Total parts = 31.
STEP 2 · Compute C C = 5500 × 15/31 ≈ Rs. 2661.29.
AnswerC ≈ Rs. 2,661.29

Module 03 · Equal Ratios

When two ratios
are equal.

Proportion is the assertion that two ratios are equal. The classical name carries algebraic identities that occasionally win seconds, and a small family of CAT question patterns that are unmistakable once recognised.

3.1The definition

Proportion Four quantities a, b, c, d are in proportion if
a : b = c : d, equivalently a/b = c/d, equivalently ad = bc

a and d are the extremes; b and c are the means.
Product of means = Product of extremes.

3.2The continued proportion and the mean proportional

If three quantities a, b, c satisfy a/b = b/c (the middle quantity equals itself), they are said to be in continued proportion. The middle quantity b is called the mean proportional between a and c, and:

Mean Proportional If a, b, c are in continued proportion, then
b² = ac, so b = √(ac)

The mean proportional is the geometric mean.

Worked: Find the mean proportional between 4 and 16. b = √(4 × 16) = √64 = 8. Sanity check: 4 : 8 = 8 : 16 = 1 : 2 ✓.

3.3Fourth proportional and third proportional

Two more named quantities that CAT occasionally tests:

  • Fourth proportional to a, b, c is the d such that a : b = c : d. So d = bc/a.
  • Third proportional to a, b is the c such that a : b = b : c. So c = b²/a.

Worked: Third proportional to 4 and 8 is 8²/4 = 16. Check: 4 : 8 = 8 : 16 ✓.

3.4Worked applications

Mean proportional Easy

Find the mean proportional between 0.32 and 0.18.

METHOD Mean proportional = √(0.32 × 0.18) = √0.0576 = 0.24.
Answer0.24
Componendo-dividendo trick Medium

Given (3x + 5y)/(3x − 5y) = 7/3, find x/y.

METHOD The form (a + b)/(a − b) suggests componendo-dividendo applied in reverse.
If (a + b)/(a − b) = 7/3, then a/b = (7 + 3)/(7 − 3) = 10/4 = 5/2.
So 3x/(5y) = 5/2 ⟹ x/y = 25/6.
Answerx/y = 25/6

Teaching note: the same problem solved by cross-multiplication takes four lines. Componendo-dividendo collapses it to one.

3.5The standard proportion check

To check if four numbers a, b, c, d are in proportion, compute ad and bc. If equal, they are.

Example: Are 4, 6, 14, 21 in proportion? Compute 4 × 21 = 84 and 6 × 14 = 84. Equal ⟹ yes, 4 : 6 = 14 : 21 (both equal 2 : 3).

Module 04 · Functional Relationships

Direct, inverse,
joint variation.

When two quantities move in a predictable functional relationship — they multiply each other, or are inversely proportional, or one depends on two others. The three patterns and how CAT disguises them.

4.1Direct variation

Y varies directly as X means Y/X is constant. Doubling X doubles Y. The relationship is linear through the origin.

Direct Variation Y ∝ X, or equivalently Y = kX for some constant k.
So Y₁/X₁ = Y₂/X₂ = k.
Cross-multiplying: Y₁ × X₂ = Y₂ × X₁.

Common contexts: cost ∝ quantity, weight ∝ volume, distance ∝ time at constant speed. Sometimes Y varies as a power of X — Y ∝ X² means Y = kX², and doubling X quadruples Y.

4.2Inverse variation

Y varies inversely as X means Y × X is constant. Doubling X halves Y. The relationship is a rectangular hyperbola.

Inverse Variation Y ∝ 1/X, or equivalently Y = k/X, or YX = k.
So Y₁ × X₁ = Y₂ × X₂ = k.

Common contexts: number of workers ∝ 1/time (more workers, less time). Pressure ∝ 1/volume at constant temperature. Speed ∝ 1/time at fixed distance.

4.3Joint variation

Y depends on more than one variable, multiplicatively. Most common form: Y ∝ XZ (Y is jointly proportional to X and Z), so Y = kXZ. Or Y ∝ X/Z (directly with X, inversely with Z).

Joint Variation Y ∝ X × Z means Y = kXZ
Y ∝ X/Z means Y = kX/Z

Common contexts: Volume of a box ∝ length × breadth × height. Area of a triangle ∝ base × height. Force ∝ mass × acceleration.

4.4The standard CAT variation problem

Almost every CAT variation problem has the same structure: a functional relationship is stated; one set of values is given; a second set is requested.

Direct variation with a power Easy

The cost of a diamond varies directly as the square of its weight. A diamond weighing 6 grams costs Rs. 7,200. What is the cost of a diamond weighing 10 grams?

STEP 1 · Set up Cost = k × weight². 7200 = k × 36 ⟹ k = 200.
STEP 2 · Apply to new weight New cost = 200 × 100 = Rs. 20,000.
AnswerRs. 20,000

4.5The diamond-breaking classic

Diamond breaks into pieces — classic variation problem Hard

The value of a diamond is proportional to the square of its weight. A diamond weighing 10 grams accidentally breaks into three pieces whose weights are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 2. The total loss in value due to the breakage is Rs. 9,000. What was the original value of the diamond?

STEP 1 · Set up Value = k × weight². Original value = k × 100 = 100k.
The three pieces have weights in ratio 1 : 2 : 2 with total 5 parts = 10 g. So pieces weigh 2 g, 4 g, 4 g.
Total new value = k(4 + 16 + 16) = 36k.
STEP 2 · The loss Loss = 100k − 36k = 64k = 9000 ⟹ k = 140.625.
STEP 3 · Original value 100k = 14,062.50.
AnswerRs. 14,062.50

Teaching note: the key insight is that value is conserved only if weight is conserved AND the value-weight relationship is linear. Since value goes as weight², breaking the diamond reduces total value even though total weight is unchanged.

Module 05 · Three and More

Multi-quantity
ratios.

Three-, four-, even five-quantity ratios. The chaining technique, partnership profit-sharing, and the elite CAT 2024 fruit-seller problem that uses multi-quantity ratio thinking.

5.1The chaining technique — recap

From Module 2: combining two two-quantity ratios requires scaling them to share a common middle term. Worked: A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5 combine to A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15.

5.2Beyond three — chains of four or more

Extends naturally. If A : B = 1 : 2, B : C = 3 : 4, and C : D = 5 : 6, find A : B : C : D.

Method: scale step by step.
A : B = 1 : 2. Multiply by 3: A : B = 3 : 6.
B : C = 3 : 4. Multiply by 2: B : C = 6 : 8.
So A : B : C = 3 : 6 : 8.
C : D = 5 : 6. Multiply by 8/5: C : D = 8 : 48/5. Or scale all by 5: A : B : C : D = 15 : 30 : 40 : 48.

5.3Partnership and profit-sharing

When two or more people invest in a business, profits are typically split in proportion to (capital invested) × (time invested). The CAT setup: two partners invest different amounts for different durations; how should profit be split?

Partnership Profit Ratio Profit share of each partner ∝ Capital × Time
Profit ratio = (C₁ × T₁) : (C₂ × T₂) : …
Partnership — different start times Medium

A invests Rs. 30,000 in a business. After 4 months, B joins with Rs. 40,000. At the end of the year, the total profit is Rs. 22,400. What is A's share?

STEP 1 · Capital × Time A: 30,000 × 12 = 3,60,000.
B: 40,000 × 8 = 3,20,000.
Ratio = 36 : 32 = 9 : 8.
STEP 2 · A's share A = 22,400 × 9/17 ≈ Rs. 11,858.82.
AnswerA's share ≈ Rs. 11,859

5.4The CAT 2023 coin-collector problem

CAT 2023 — coin collectors with integer constraints Elite

The number of coins collected per week by two coin-collectors A and B are in the ratio 3 : 4. If the total number of coins collected by A in 5 weeks is a multiple of 7, and the total number of coins collected by B in 3 weeks is a multiple of 24, then the minimum possible number of coins collected by A in one week is:

STEP 1 · k-framing Let A = 3x per week, B = 4x per week (where x is a positive integer).
STEP 2 · A's constraint A's 5-week total = 15x. For 15x to be divisible by 7, since gcd(15, 7) = 1, we need x divisible by 7.
STEP 3 · B's constraint B's 3-week total = 12x. For 12x divisible by 24, we need x divisible by 2.
STEP 4 · LCM x must be divisible by lcm(7, 2) = 14. Minimum x = 14. So A's weekly coins = 3 × 14 = 42.
Answer42

Teaching note: this problem perfectly illustrates the multiplier framing. The "common multiplier" k captures the unknown scale, and the integer constraints fix k via divisibility logic.

5.5The CAT 2024 fruit-seller problem

CAT 2024 — fruit-seller's 187 fruits Elite

A fruit seller has a total of 187 fruits consisting of apples, mangoes and oranges. The number of apples and mangoes are in the ratio 5 : 2. After she sells 75 apples, 26 mangoes and half of the oranges, the ratio of number of unsold apples to number of unsold oranges becomes 3 : 2. The total number of unsold fruits is:

STEP 1 · k-framing Apples = 5x, Mangoes = 2x, Oranges = 187 − 7x.
STEP 2 · Unsold quantities Apples left = 5x − 75. Mangoes left = 2x − 26. Oranges left = (187 − 7x)/2.
STEP 3 · The new ratio (5x − 75) / ((187 − 7x)/2) = 3/2
2(5x − 75) / (187 − 7x) = 3/2
4(5x − 75) = 3(187 − 7x)
20x − 300 = 561 − 21x
41x = 861
x = 21.
STEP 4 · Compute unsold total Apples left = 5(21) − 75 = 30. Mangoes left = 2(21) − 26 = 16. Oranges left = (187 − 147)/2 = 20.
Total = 30 + 16 + 20 = 66.
Answer66 fruits

Module 06 · Applications

Where ratios
actually live
in CAT.

Eight applied themes — partnership and profit sharing, ages, mixtures (re-met from Vol. IV), salaries, work and time, ratio shifts after events, integer-constrained ratio problems, and the hidden ratios in geometry.

6.1Theme — Partnership and profit sharing

Already met in Module 5. Profit ratio = (Capital × Time) ratio. Variants include partners who join late, leave early, or change their investment mid-year.

6.2Theme — Ages and time

The most common ratio archetype in mocks. Two people's ages now in some ratio; some years ago (or hence) in another ratio. Always solvable by k-framing and one or two equations.

CAT 2024 — Rajesh and Garima's ages Hard

When Rajesh's age was the same as Garima's present age, the ratio of their ages was 3 : 2. When Garima's age becomes the same as Rajesh's present age, what will be the ratio of Rajesh's age to Garima's age?

STEP 1 · Set up variables Let Rajesh's present age = R, Garima's = G.
"When Rajesh's age was G" — this was R − G years ago. At that time Rajesh was G; Garima was G − (R − G) = 2G − R.
The ratio Rajesh : Garima at that time was G : (2G − R) = 3 : 2.
So 2G = 3(2G − R) ⟹ 2G = 6G − 3R ⟹ 3R = 4G ⟹ R/G = 4/3. So R = 4k, G = 3k for some k.
STEP 2 · "When Garima's age becomes R" That's R − G = 4k − 3k = k years from now.
At that time, Rajesh will be R + k = 5k. Garima will be R = 4k.
Ratio = 5k : 4k = 5 : 4.
Answer5 : 4

6.3Theme — Mixtures revisited

From Volume IV. Every mixture problem is a ratio problem. Now you have the ratio toolkit and the conservation principle. They unite cleanly.

6.4Theme — Salary and income splits

Salary ratio after increment Medium

The salaries of A and B are in the ratio 4 : 7. If A's salary increases by 25% and B's salary increases by 20%, what is the new ratio?

METHOD Take A = 400, B = 700. New A = 500. New B = 840. Ratio = 500 : 840 = 25 : 42.
Answer25 : 42

6.5Theme — Work and time (preview of Volume VII)

If A and B together complete a task in a given time, their work-rates are inversely proportional to the time. Volume VII will treat this in full. Worth previewing here that the inverse-variation logic of Module 4 is the structural engine.

6.6Theme — Ratio shifts after events

"Original ratio 3 : 4; A spends some, ratio becomes 1 : 2." Solve with k-framing and one event-equation.

6.7Theme — Integer-constrained ratios

The CAT 2023 coin-collector problem we solved in Module 5. The setup is a ratio with both quantities required to be integers and satisfying additional divisibility constraints. The technique: parametrise with k, apply each constraint as a divisibility condition on k, find the LCM.

6.8Theme — Hidden ratios in geometry

Two similar triangles have all corresponding sides in the same ratio. Areas scale as the square of that ratio. Volumes scale as the cube. This is direct variation in disguise and an essential bridge to geometry (Volume XI).

Module 07 · Tactics

Twelve tips,
tricks, and traps.

The accumulated wisdom — what to drill, what to whisper, and the predictable mistakes you'll see in mocks again and again.

TIP 01 · K-MULTIPLIER
Always write A = 3k, not A = 3

The k-framing is the single most important habit in this chapter. Every problem becomes a linear equation in k.

TIP 02 · SIMPLIFY
Reduce ratios before computing

12 : 18 → 2 : 3 before doing anything else. Small numbers prevent arithmetic errors.

TIP 03 · MATCH
To combine ratios, match common terms

For A : B and B : C, scale to make B equal in both. LCM of the B-values is the cleanest approach.

TIP 04 · NEVER ADD
Ratios cannot be added directly

(A + C) : (B + D) is NOT determined by A:B and C:D. You need absolute values.

TIP 05 · MEAN PROP
Mean proportional is √(ac)

For continued proportion a, b, c, the middle b satisfies b² = ac. Spot this pattern.

TIP 06 · POWERS
Direct variation can be Y ∝ Xn

If Y ∝ X², doubling X quadruples Y. Watch for "varies as the square of" or "as the cube of" in problem stems.

TIP 07 · INVERSE
Inverse variation means product constant

Y × X = k. When X doubles, Y halves. Common in workers-and-time and pressure-volume problems.

TIP 08 · PARTNERSHIP
Profit ratio = Capital × Time

Standard partnership rule. Compute each partner's (capital × time) product and take the ratio.

TIP 09 · INTEGER
Integer constraints become divisibility

If A = 3x and A must be a multiple of 7, then x must be a multiple of 7 (since gcd(3, 7) = 1).

TIP 10 · COMP-DIV
(a+b)/(a−b) form invites componendo-dividendo

When CAT gives this exact form, the identity (a+b)/(a−b) = (c+d)/(c−d) collapses three lines of algebra to one.

TIP 11 · ORDER
A : B is not B : A

Read the problem statement carefully. Re-read to confirm the order of the requested answer.

TIP 12 · VERIFY
Plug back into the original constraint

After solving for k, substitute back to check the ratio still holds. Catches sign errors.

7.1Five traps

Trap 1 — Adding ratios. "A : B = 2 : 3 and C : D = 4 : 5. Find (A+C) : (B+D)." Students answer 6 : 8 = 3 : 4. Wrong, unless A=C and B=D in absolute terms, which is not given.

Trap 2 — Reversing the direction of variation. "Y varies inversely as X" doesn't mean Y = −X or Y = 1 − X. It means Y = k/X.

Trap 3 — Forgetting the power. "Varies as the square of" means Y = kX². Treating it as Y = kX gives wrong answers in elite variation problems.

Trap 4 — Wrong order in answer. Problem asks "A's share to B's share", student gives B : A. Always re-read.

Trap 5 — Mismatched scaling in multi-quantity ratios. Trying to combine A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5 by lining them up directly. Must rescale to match B.

7.2The two-minute checklist

  1. Read twice. Identify the quantities and the relationship.
  2. Write each ratio in k-multiplier form (A = 3k, B = 4k, etc.).
  3. Translate every additional condition into an equation in k.
  4. For multi-quantity ratios, chain by matching common terms.
  5. For variation, identify direct vs inverse, set Y = kX (or k/X), solve for k.
  6. Plug k back; verify the original ratio holds.
  7. Re-read the question; answer in the order asked.

Q8 · Ages backward and forwardMedium

5 years ago, the ratio of A's age to B's age was 3 : 4. 5 years hence, it will be 5 : 6. Find their present ages.

SETUPLet present ages be A and B.

Q17 · Three-number ratio with constraintHard

A diamond worth Rs. 200,000 (whose value varies as the square of its weight) breaks into three pieces in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3. Find the loss in value.

9.1Core definitions

Ratio & Multiplier A : B = a : b means A = ak, B = bk for some k > 0.
Total parts = a + b. Each part = total/(a+b).
Proportion a : b = c : d ⟺ ad = bc
Mean proportional of a, c: b = √(ac)
Fourth proportional to a, b, c: d = bc/a

9.2Variation identities

Variation Formulas Direct: Y = kX · Y/X = constant
Inverse: Y = k/X · YX = constant
Joint: Y = kXZ or Y = kX/Z
Power: Y = kXn

9.3Manipulating ratios

  • To combine A : B and B : C, scale both to have the same value of B (LCM trick).
  • Ratios cannot be added directly.
  • Scaling both sides by the same factor preserves the ratio.

9.4The classical properties

Properties of Equal Ratios Alternendo: a/c = b/d
Invertendo: b/a = d/c
Componendo: (a + b)/b = (c + d)/d
Dividendo: (a − b)/b = (c − d)/d
Comp-Div: (a + b)/(a − b) = (c + d)/(c − d)

9.5Partnership formula

Profit Sharing Profit ratio ∝ Capital × Time
Share of partner i = Total profit × (Cᵢ × Tᵢ) / Σ (CⱼTⱼ)

9.6The traps catalogue

  • Adding ratios is not allowed in general.
  • "Varies as the square of X" means kX², not kX.
  • Inverse variation means YX = k, not Y = −X.
  • Order matters: A : B ≠ B : A.
  • For integer-constrained ratios, parametrise and use divisibility.
